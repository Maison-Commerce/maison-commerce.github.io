A/B Testing Roadmap
Conversion research · Netherlands · Denmark · Sweden

Market-by-market CRO growth plan

A market-by-market plan to grow conversion across the Dreaver NL, DK and SE stores. Seven evidence-backed tests, from the homepage and collection through to the product page, each with a problem, solution, hypothesis and a mobile before/after wireframe, judged against first-party commerce data. Every idea is tested in its home market first, and if it wins there it rolls out to the other two.

Before anything else

Clarify the target

The whole plan hinges on the growth target, and right now "+1 point" is ambiguous. Before we commit, confirm with the client whether they mean +1 percentage point (for example 1.9% to 2.9%) or +1% relative (1.9% to about 1.92%). The two are worlds apart, so we should not accept the framing silently.

If "+1 point" means +1 percentage pointNLDKSE
Current conversion rate1.90%1.33%0.99%
+1pp target2.90%2.33%1.99%
That is a relative lift of+53%+75%+101%

Worth saying plainly

On these baselines, +1 percentage point is a 50% to 100% relative lift. That is not a CRO programme outcome in a single quarter; it is a site rebuild plus a change in traffic mix. At today's traffic it would be worth on the order of €1M+ in added revenue per quarter across the three stores, which is a business-plan target, not a testing target. Naming that gap now keeps the conversation constructive.

What this test programme can realistically deliver

A well-run test programme typically lifts conversion by 10% to 20% relative over two to three quarters, market by market. These are planning bands to align on, not guarantees, shown at today's traffic and average order value.

Data insights · Shopify + survey

Three stores, three problems

We have three stores and, really, three different problems. The conversion gap is clear (NL 1.90%, DK 1.33%, SE 0.99%), but each market loses people at a different spot, and the direction each store is heading matters just as much as the number. Below: the funnel side by side, what's happening in each market, the patterns they share, and what it all adds up to.

Rates are % of sessions, for Jun 6 to Sep 4 2026. Est. checkout completion = completed checkouts ÷ reached-checkout sessions. DK and SE log slightly more checkout sessions than cart sessions, so the steps aren't a clean sequence. Check Shopify's definitions before over-reading them.

True in every store

Discounting is already heavy (around 30% of gross sales everywhere) yet DK and SE still convert poorly, so more discount isn't the answer; helping people understand the value is. And traffic is almost entirely mobile (82% NL, 92% DK, 94% SE), so mobile is where testing counts.

One shared bright spot: reviews are the number-one reason people choose Dreaver in all three markets (60 to 71%), the most dependable lever we have.

Each market
NL

Strong store, shrinking

The efficient store: best conversion, best checkout completion, and the only rising returning-customer rate. The trouble is upstream.

  • Add-to-cart and checkout reach both around 6.5%; checkout completion 29.5%, the best of the three.
  • But sessions are down 34%, orders 20% and sales 23%, and paid-social revenue is falling hard.
  • Buyers still name price most (43.2%) and ask about fit, folding, noise and installation.
PriorityKeep the checkout strong and put the energy into traffic volume and acquisition quality.
DK

Engaged, then stalls

The early funnel looks like NL, but the fewest checkouts convert. The gap is at the finish, not on the product page.

  • Add-to-cart 5.97% and checkout reach 6.24% are close to NL, but only 21.4% of checkouts complete.
  • 39.5% of leavers aren't sure they'll actually use the treadmill; delivery is slower than NL (3 to 6 days vs 2 to 4).
  • Consistent with friction at the final step: payment, delivery expectations, mobile checkout.
PriorityAudit the checkout by device, payment method and shipping step before more product-page work.
SE

Small, but growing fast

Loses people earliest and converts the least, yet it's expanding across the board. Price dominates the reasons people leave.

  • Lowest add-to-cart (4.18%) and CVR (0.99%), but sessions up 35%, orders up 63%, sales up 59%.
  • Price is the top exit reason (61.6%); many shoppers compare other brands.
  • Only 16.9% of buyers say price nearly stopped them, so this reads as selection, not a resolved concern.
PriorityMake package value and model fit clear before add-to-cart, then check traffic quality.
Patterns across all three
Trend

NL and DK are shrinking, SE is growing

Judging Sweden on its 0.99% rate alone is unfair, because a fast-broadening top of funnel pulls the rate down while revenue climbs. And NL's strong 1.90% sits on a shrinking base. Direction matters as much as level.

Discounting

Heavy everywhere, and not the lever

Discounts run 29 to 31% of gross in all three stores, yet DK and SE still convert poorly. Deeper cuts aren't the fix; helping people understand what the package is worth is the cheaper, testable path.

Product mix

Elevate Pro earns the most, but tests target Motion Pro

Elevate Pro is the top revenue product in all three stores, with Motion Pro second. Any winning product-page test should move to the Elevate Pro page too.

Entry points

Discovery tests sit where traffic lands

The treadmill collection and the Motion Pro and Elevate Pro pages are the top landing pages in every market, so the homepage-route and collection-filter tests address real entry volume.

The takeaways that matter

  1. One conversion number hides three problems. NL is shrinking demand on a strong site, DK is a checkout leak, SE is top-of-funnel plus growth dilution. Handle them separately.
  2. NL's real risk is traffic, not conversion. Protect what's working and dig into the decline.
  3. DK needs a checkout audit before more product-page tests. Engagement is fine; the final step is where it drops.
  4. Sweden is growing, so don't judge it on rate alone. Lead with value and model choice.
  5. Discounts are already ~30% of gross and aren't moving the needle. Test value clarity, not bigger cuts.
  6. Traffic is 82 to 94% mobile. Make mobile the default test surface and pull device-level CVR to see how mobile and desktop really differ.
  7. Reviews are the top purchase reason in all three markets. The most dependable lever we have.
  8. Elevate Pro out-earns Motion Pro but the tests target Motion Pro. Extend any winner to the Elevate Pro page.

Worth remembering: Shopify covers 90 days while the surveys cover only a few days at the end, so don't multiply survey shares by traffic. Cross-market euro figures use rough rates for scale only. The cleanest next step is to break Shopify down by market × landing page × device × channel × new/returning × checkout step × payment method to confirm where each problem really starts.

Survey insights · Comparison

What the exit & buyer surveys say

Six exit-intent and post-purchase surveys across NL, DK and SE. Substantial differences are worth investigating, but most are directional — not statistically established across these small samples. Reviews are the leading reason for choosing Dreaver in every market.

How certain are we?

After correcting for all 132 pairwise comparisons (Fisher exact + Holm), only Sweden's higher "cannot remember" recall for time-to-purchase survives correction against both NL and DK. That is a difference in reported recall, not evidence of a faster or slower buying journey. Price, usage and fitness differences are directional — useful for prioritising research, not for claiming why conversion differs.

The one statistically robust difference: recall, not purchase speed

Sweden's "cannot remember" share for time-from-awareness-to-purchase is 52.8%, vs 9.5% (DK) and 2.3% (NL) — +50.5pp over NL and +43.3pp over DK, both passing Holm correction (adjusted p ≈ 0.000002–0.000007). Do not read this as a shorter Swedish buying cycle: over half of answered durations are unknown and 24 buyers didn't answer. Audit the question's wording, options, device experience and routing before using it for anything.

Method: 132 two-sided Fisher exact tests across 44 harmonised outcomes (NL–DK, NL–SE, DK–SE), Holm step-down correction at 0.05. A raw p<0.05 alone is not a significance claim in a screen this broad; failing correction is not proof the markets are identical.

Problem

Visitors may understand the benefits of a treadmill but struggle to identify which model suits their needs — so a single "shop" path leaves them to map their own use onto a model.

Solution

Add three optional intended-use routes near the homepage's main CTA. Each opens a relevant product selection with a short explanation and an option to reset; the existing shopping path is kept.

Hypothesis

IF visitors can start with their intended activity,

THEN more will purchase a suitable treadmill,

BECAUSE identifying relevant models becomes easier. Track qualified PDP visits as a diagnostic; monitor margin per visitor and model-related returns.

MC_COLL_FILTER_BY_MOVEMENT_SE

Problem

Price and availability filters don't help shoppers work out whether a treadmill supports their intended activity — they're left to interpret technical specifications themselves.

Solution

Add visible activity filter chips that update the product grid, show the matching product count and explain the selection, with a clear reset. Existing filters stay in place.

Hypothesis

IF shoppers can filter by activity instead of interpreting technical specifications,

THEN more collection visitors will purchase a suitable treadmill,

BECAUSE matching a model to their intended use becomes easier. Monitor empty results, revenue and contribution per visitor.

Execution

Recommended order

A proposed sequence, not a fixed priority. Each test runs in its home market first; a winner is then localised and re-tested in the other two markets. Tests that touch the same PDP area run separately so results stay attributable.

Phase 0 · Prerequisites

Settle before anything ships

Confirm the verified speed/app details and use-cases for T1, tag existing reviews by topic for T2, verify USP tooltip content with operations for T3, select the routine reviews and confirm provenance for T4, and lock the included-vs-optional package data for T5.

Phase 1 · Fastest PDP clarity

T3 · T5

USP tooltips (NL) and the package-value summary (SE) are low-effort content additions at the buy box. They map to the clearest evidence — return/support clarity and price-to-value — and are the quickest to build.

Phase 2 · Proof & motivation

T2 · T4 · T1

Review topic tabs (NL) and routine reviews (DK) both rely on curated authentic reviews; run them separately since both touch review content. The use-case routine module (T1, NL) needs verified capability content. Any Phase-1 or Phase-2 winner becomes a cross-market rollout candidate.

Phase 3 · Discovery & selection

T6 · T7

Homepage intended-use routes (T6) and collection activity filters (T7) help visitors find a suitable model before the PDP. They sit on a different surface, so they can run alongside the PDP tests — but run T6 and T7 sequentially or on separate audiences so their effects can be distinguished. Start in SE (T7) and SE or DK (T6). Survey findings are directional; they don't establish that selection difficulty causes the market conversion gaps.

Measurement: use unique eligible sessions per surface as the denominator; treat clicks and tooltip/tab opens as diagnostic and decide on purchase CVR, revenue, and contribution margin per eligible session. Report mobile and desktop separately. The commerce platform is the source of truth for orders and revenue.
Cross-market rule: a test that wins in its home market is re-run in the other two markets with localised copy, currency and process, and re-validated against local evidence before launch.

Wireframes: styling (palette, type, price treatment, layout) is taken from the live Dreaver PDP; copy is English reference. Numbers, ratings and section labels are schematic examples — validate every assumption, localise the copy and finalise imagery and spacing against the target storefront before build.
