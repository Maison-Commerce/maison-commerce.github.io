Measurement rules

  1. Evaluate every test on sessions that reach this PDP. Do not use blended sitewide conversion as the denominator.
  2. Mobile is the decision-making segment — approximately 96% of sessions are mobile.
  3. For persuasion and UX tests, use mobile PDP-to-add-to-cart as the leading metric and revenue per PDP visitor as the commercial guardrail.
  4. For pricing and offer tests, prioritise contribution profit per PDP visitor; fall back to revenue per PDP visitor only if contribution data is unavailable.
  5. Segment by advertorial/Meta versus direct traffic and by new versus returning visitors where sample size permits.
  6. Read shade-fit, guarantee, virtual-preview and expectation-setting tests through the full return window.
  7. For the adjacent collection-page subscription experiment, use eligible identified prior purchasers as the denominator and read net contribution through at least the second billing. Do not blend that result with PDP experiments.
Research basis

What the audit and the research found

This roadmap combines live mobile inspection of the current PDP, Shopify quantitative analysis, new- and returning-customer survey analysis, competitor PDP research, moderated usability-test findings, and stakeholder input on revenue strategy and test feasibility. The two supplied survey-insight files are duplicates and are treated as one evidence source.

Live-page findings that shaped the roadmap

  1. Scratch-to-Win appears very early on a first eligible exposure and is subject to session/frequency rules.
  2. One bottle is already selected by default.
  3. Selecting two bottles changes the bundle card to $49 while the main product price and sticky card remain at $39. This is prerequisite QA, not a treatment.
  4. A sticky mobile purchase card already exists, but its CTA says “Shop Now” and scrolls back to the offer rather than adding the selected pack.
  5. The guarantee, mechanism explanation, mature-skin testimonials, before/afters and results statistics already exist, but are not always placed or presented optimally.
  6. The gallery contains approximately 15+ assets.
  7. The tone claim is contradictory: one area says “all skin tones”, another says fair-to-medium tones.
  8. Scarcity is intentionally retained as a revenue mechanism.
  9. The Mystery Gift is intentionally retained as part of the bundle value.

MC_PDP_VIRTUAL_FACE_PREVIEW_SMOOCHE · generated id MC_PDP_FACEPREVIEWTRYON

Problem

A shopper cannot visualise the likely finish on her own face before buying. A virtual preview could reduce that uncertainty, but it is exploratory: a camera filter may not reproduce an adaptive formula, lighting, texture or final shade precisely.

Solution

One optional “Preview it on your face” launcher near the gallery and price, without displacing the offer or primary ATC. Request camera access only after an explicit tap, with a still-photo or model-preview fallback when access is declined or unavailable. Label it clearly: “Visual preview — your result may vary with lighting, camera, screen, application and the adaptive formula.” Never call the output an exact match. Keep it non-blocking and easy to close, and lazy-load the app after intent so scripts do not slow the initial product view. Retain no image unless the shopper explicitly agrees.

Hypothesis

IF mobile shoppers can optionally preview the likely cosmetic effect on their own face,

THEN mobile PDP ATC and revenue per PDP visitor will increase,

BECAUSE personal visualisation will reduce uncertainty about how the product may look on them before purchase.

Use one credible scarcity mechanism

Scarcity stays as a revenue mechanism, but it has to communicate one real constraint. Stock pressure plus time pressure — or a resetting timer — makes the whole offer feel less credible.

Primary KPI
Contribution profit / mobile PDP visitor (revenue per visitor if contribution data is unavailable)
Secondary
Purchase CVR · mobile ATC · checkout completion · time to ATC
Guardrails
Bounce rate · cancellations and refunds · support complaints · offer-expiry failures
Targeting
Mobile PDP — /products/ccf2
Segment
Mobile visitors who reach the purchase box
Tool
Experiment platform + inventory or offer-scheduling data
Evidence
Usability test — the participant could not tell whether the message meant inventory or a deadline. Survey — timer pressure can backfire with an older, careful-reading audience.
Prerequisites
Do not launch the stock variant without synchronised sellable inventory, or the expiry variant without an enforceable deadline and a verified post-expiry state. Run separately from the purchase-reassurance test (extended edition only) and other purchase-box changes.

Test 9 in the research brief · T2 in the extended roadmap
A/B test ID
MC_PDP_SINGLE_CREDIBLE_SCARCITY_SMOOCHE · generated id MC_PDP_ONESCARCITYMECHANISM

Problem

The current module combines an abstract “89% sold” meter with a countdown, so the shopper cannot tell what is actually limited. Combining stock and time pressure — or using a resetting timer or simulated decrement — undermines the credibility of the whole offer.

Solution

Ship exactly one truthful constraint, chosen to match the real operational reality. Never display both. If traffic permits, run a four-cell design: current urgency stack vs no urgency vs genuine stock vs genuine expiry. If only an A/B is possible, compare the control with the one operationally valid mechanism.

Variant A — genuine low stock, no timer

Only when inventory is genuinely constrained. Remove the countdown, percentage and progress meter. Show an absolute inventory message below a real pre-agreed threshold (“Only 18 bottles left at this price”), pulled from actual sellable Shopify inventory or a documented promotional allocation. Refresh every 10–15 seconds and animate a reduction only when the verified response is lower — never force a decrement because time has passed. Define the unit precisely: a two-bottle order consumes two bottles. If stock rises on a restock or released reservation, update honestly or hide the module.

Variant B — genuine time-limited offer, no stock claim

Only when the price, gift or shipping offer genuinely changes at a fixed deadline. Remove the stock percentage, meter and “selling out” language. State exactly what expires and when (“$39 Summer price ends Sunday at 11:59 PM ET”). A countdown may support the explicit deadline, but the end timestamp must persist across refreshes and devices, the stated price must actually change at zero, and the deadline must not reset for a new session unless the offer is genuinely session-specific and labelled as such.

Hypothesis

IF we replace the mixed, fake-feeling scarcity stack with one concrete and truthful constraint,

THEN contribution profit and purchase CVR per PDP visitor will increase,

BECAUSE shoppers can understand what is limited without reconciling competing urgency claims or distrusting a resetting mechanism.

MC_PDP_VERIFIED_BUYER_CAPTIONS_SMOOCHE · generated id MC_PDP_IDENTITYLEDCAPTIONS

Problem

Some customer imagery reads as synthetic, and that perception spills onto the whole offer. Directly defending an image with “Real Photo” or “No AI” can introduce or amplify the concern rather than resolve it.

Solution

Add one compact identity-led caption to qualifying customer images — for example “Karen, 49 · Verified Purchase”. Use the approved first name or first name plus last initial, age with explicit permission and documented accuracy, and “Verified Purchase” only when the image links to a genuine order in the source system. A tap may open the full review with skin concern, supported tone/undertone and specific result. Position the caption away from the face and visible skin texture. No defensive authenticity language. Images, gallery order, crop, copy and offer stay unchanged.

Hypothesis

IF we identify people in customer imagery by approved name, age and verified-purchase status,

THEN mobile PDP ATC and revenue per PDP visitor will increase,

BECAUSE shoppers will perceive the result as attributable to a specific customer rather than anonymous or overly manufactured marketing creative.

A/B test ID
MC_PDP_IS_THIS_RIGHT_FOR_ME_SMOOCHE · generated id MC_PDP_SUITABILITYBELOWATC

Problem

The PDP contains benefits, tone guidance, application advice and risk reversal, but the answers are distributed across a long mobile page. At the purchase decision, shoppers still have to decide whether the product fits their skin goals and what result is realistic.

Solution

One concise section immediately below the primary ATC and purchase reassurance, titled “Is This Product Right for Me?”, structured around self-selection. “It may be right for you if you want”: a more even, radiant, younger-looking appearance without a heavy mask-like finish; natural, buildable coverage that softens visible redness and imperfections; a simpler shade-selection experience within the verified supported range; a skin-like finish designed for mature-skin preparation and application. “Know what to expect”: the verified coverage level, finish, adaptation time, wear expectation and supported tone range; a clear statement that makeup softens the appearance of uneven tone and fine lines but will not erase wrinkles or natural texture; a link to the mature-skin preparation and application guide; and the policy-accurate guarantee with a route to full terms. Key points visible and concise on mobile, supporting detail in progressively disclosed rows.

Hypothesis

IF we place a concise suitability and realistic-expectations section directly below the ATC,

THEN refund-adjusted revenue per PDP visitor and purchase CVR will increase,

BECAUSE skeptical shoppers can quickly confirm the product fits their goals, understand its limits, and buy with more accurate expectations.

Prove the shade match across real supported skin tones

The current tone spectrum is passive and abstract. It does not answer “what will this look like on someone with my tone and mature skin?” — and shoppers who have repeatedly bought the wrong shade will not infer fit from a colour strip.

Primary KPI
Mobile PDP→ATC (intent-to-treat)
Secondary
Revenue / PDP visitor · mosaic interactions · purchase CVR · sticky-ATC use after module exposure
Guardrails
Shade-related returns, refunds and support contacts
Targeting
Mobile PDP — /products/ccf2
Segment
Shade-anxious mobile visitors
Tool
Experiment platform
Evidence
Wrong shade was a prior-foundation frustration for 34% of surveyed buyers. “Perfect match” was the second-strongest ad element with 2,725 selections. Shade match was valued by 46% of returning respondents.
Prerequisites
Resolve the “all skin tones” vs “fair-to-medium” contradiction and document one substantiated supported range before launch. Verify image provenance and licensing. Do not run concurrently with T5, the gallery-reduction test (extended edition only) or T3.
Brief ref
Test 5 in the research brief · T10 in the extended roadmap
A/B test ID
MC_PDP_SHADE_PROOF_REAL_TONES_SMOOCHE · generated id MC_PDP_SHADEPROOFMOSAIC

Problem

The current tone spectrum is passive and abstract. Shoppers who have repeatedly bought the wrong shade need to see the formula on real people. “Suntan anxiety” was not directly measured, but the proof should visibly demonstrate that the finish blends into the wearer’s existing tone rather than making the face look artificially darker.

Solution

Replace the static spectrum in its current location with a compact two-column mosaic of four to six real mature users across the accurately supported tone range. Each tile: a real customer or permissioned model; before, application and finished-result evidence rather than a portrait alone; tone and undertone label; age and relevant skin concern where permitted; consistent lighting, angle and white balance with visible skin texture; and brief guidance on the expected finish. Heading: “See it adapt on real skin tones”.

T7A — prove the content in its current location

Swap the abstract spectrum for the real-user mosaic without moving it. Do not use “all skin tones”, “any skin colour” or tanning/seasonal-adaptation claims unless the product has been validated for them.

Follow-up 5B — test the below-ATC placement

If the mosaic wins, move the identical winning module directly below the primary ATC and risk-reversal line without changing its images, labels or copy. This isolates placement from content. Do not test new content and a new placement at the same time.

Hypothesis

IF shoppers can see controlled results on real mature users across Smooche’s verified supported tone range,

THEN mobile PDP ATC and refund-adjusted revenue will increase,

BECAUSE they can evaluate personal fit and confirm the formula blends with the wearer’s tone rather than relying on an abstract or universal claim.

ACollectionP3 exploratoryHigh effort

Offer an optional subscription to identified returning purchasers

Outside the core PDP roadmap: a separate page, a separate eligible audience and a long-term metric. It must not be blended with the PDP experiments.

Primary KPI
60/90-day net contribution per eligible returning visitor, through at least the second billing
Secondary
Repeat-purchase CVR · subscription enrolment · AOV · second-shipment retention
Guardrails
One-time purchase cannibalisation · collection CVR · pre-second-shipment cancellations · refunds · chargebacks · support contacts · gross margin
Targeting
Relevant collection page / replenishment collection
Segment
Identified prior foundation purchasers approaching a validated depletion window
Tool
Subscription app + experiment platform + customer data
Evidence
Returning-customer satisfaction is strong in the survey sample while repeat purchasing is thin. Only ~6% of returning respondents came back because they ran out and restocked, and first-time buyers explicitly feared hidden subscriptions. Demand skews toward better bundles, larger sizes and refills.
Prerequisites
Do not target generic cookie-based returning browsers. Eligibility requires an identified prior foundation purchase, no active subscription, and elapsed time near an evidence-based depletion window. Confirm demand and consumption timing through order-gap analysis or a structured returning-customer survey before launch. Use eligible identified prior purchasers as the denominator and read net contribution through at least the second billing.
Brief ref
Adjacent Test A in the research brief · A in the extended roadmap
A/B test ID
MC_COLL_OPTIONAL_SUBSCRIPTION_RETURNING_SMOOCHE · generated id MC_COLL_RETURNINGSUBSCRIPTION

Problem

Returning-customer satisfaction is strong while repeat purchasing is thin. A carefully targeted replenishment offer could make repeat purchasing easier for satisfied buyers, but the idea is not supported strongly enough for new visitors, who explicitly fear hidden subscriptions.

Solution

Add one clearly optional “Subscribe & Save” product card to the relevant collection page for eligible customers only. Keep one-time purchase visible and selected by default. Show the recurring price, discount, delivery cadence, next-charge timing and what is included before the customer clicks. Let the customer choose a cadence rather than forcing an interval. State “Skip, pause, or cancel” only when each action is operationally available and easy. Do not show the card to anonymous or new visitors in the first experiment.

Interaction note

If subscriptions become available, the purchase-reassurance test (extended edition only) must stay offer-specific: “This selected pack is a one-time purchase · no recurring charge.” It must not imply that Smooche never offers subscriptions elsewhere.

Hypothesis

IF identified past purchasers approaching depletion see a transparent, optional subscription product on the relevant collection page,

THEN 60/90-day net contribution per eligible returning visitor will increase,

BECAUSE satisfied customers gain a convenient, structured way to replenish without confusing first-time buyers or removing one-time purchase choice.

Execution

Recommended sequencing

The agreed run order for the shortlist, not a priority ranking. Four tests from the extended roadmap were removed, so the constraints below cover only what actually ships here — where a surviving constraint still refers to a removed test, that test is named in full.

Phase 0 · Prerequisite QA — not experiments

Fix these before the sequence starts

Synchronise the selected pack and price across the buy box, sticky bar, cart payload and cart — required before T6. Document one substantiated supported tone range and resolve the “all tones” versus “fair-to-medium” contradiction; this sits in Phase 0 because the order puts T1 ahead of T7. Reconcile the 30-day guarantee with the separate 14-day/new-and-unused language before T4. Verify customer identities, ages, purchase status and image consent before T3, and any live inventory or offer-expiry data before T2. None of this is a test variant.

Sequence 1 · T1 – T4

T1 · T2 · T3 · T4

T1 virtual face preview — ships only after output, privacy, compatibility and performance validation, and after Phase 0 has documented the supported tone range. T2 one credible scarcity mechanism, run separately from T6 and any other purchase-box change. T3 verified-buyer captions on a frozen image set — not concurrent with T5 or T7, which also alter proof imagery. T4 the illustrated suitability section below the ATC, kept clear of T1 and T7B.

Sequence 2 · T5 – T7

T5 · T6 · T7

T5 mature-skin transformation as the first gallery slide. T6 direct sticky Add to Cart, launched only after price-correctness QA. T7A proves real-user tone content in place and T7B then moves the winning module below the ATC, so content and placement stay separable.

Adjacent returning-customer lane

Test A

Test A runs only after replenishment cadence and demand validation. It uses a separate page, a separate eligible audience and a long-term metric, so it sits outside the PDP sequence and its result is never blended with the PDP experiments.

Interaction notes

  1. The order puts T1 ahead of T7. The brief made the virtual preview conditional on real-user tone proof establishing a credible supported range. That prerequisite has moved into Phase 0 as documentation work — T1 must not launch until the substantiated range is signed off, even though T7 runs later.
  2. T3 must use the exact same approved imagery in control and variant. Do not run it alongside T5 or T7, which alter the same images, order or proof surface.
  3. Do not run T7B, T1 and T4 concurrently — each addresses fit confidence near or immediately below the ATC.
  4. Run T7A before T7B so proof content and placement effects stay separable.
  5. Fix selected-pack and price synchronisation before T6. Price correctness is prerequisite QA, not a variant.
  6. Run T2 separately from T6 and any other purchase-box change, so urgency and purchase-action effects stay interpretable.
  7. Verify identity, age, purchase status, licensing and consent before T3; validate camera consent, data retention, output accuracy and app performance before T1; substantiate live stock or offer expiry before T2.
  8. Resolve inaccurate shade claims before T1, T4 and T7, and contradictory guarantee terms before T4.
  9. T4 may intentionally reduce low-fit purchases — evaluate it on refund-adjusted revenue or contribution through the return window, not ATC alone.
  10. Removed from this edition: the Scratch-to-Win timing test, the gallery-reduction test, the proof-system audit and its badge-source follow-up, and the purchase-reassurance test.
