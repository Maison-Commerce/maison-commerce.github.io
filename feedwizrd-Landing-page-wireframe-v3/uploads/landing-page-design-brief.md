# Feedwizrd Responsive SaaS Landing Page Design Brief

> Brief for one responsive, conversion-focused SaaS landing page. It defines the page strategy, required sections, content hierarchy, responsive behavior, proof needs, and conversion requirements. It does not define the brand identity or final visual system.

**Prepared:** 27 August 2026  
**Page type:** Single-page SaaS acquisition lander  
**Primary audience:** Shopify dropshippers running Google Ads  
**Working public starting price:** $29/month; exact store allocation must be confirmed  
**Primary page objective:** Convert qualified visitors into Feedwizrd signups  
**Secondary objective:** Let visitors understand the product before granting Shopify or Google access

## 1. Strategic Direction

### The page should sell a decision loop

Competitors already promise profit dashboards. Feedwizrd should not present itself as another place to look at numbers. The page should show a continuous daily workflow:

> **Find the profit leak → improve or remove the product → monitor what changed.**

This connects the brand's three strongest capabilities:

1. Product-level profit visibility
2. AI-supported feed and campaign actions
3. Feed-change monitoring over time

### Working positioning

Feedwizrd is the daily product-profit control center for Shopify dropshippers running Google Ads. It shows which products are actually making money, helps the merchant take an approved action, and tracks what happens afterward.

**Validation condition:** This position depends on the product being able to connect a feed change to later performance in a meaningful way.

### Core conversion hypothesis

If Shopify dropshippers can see a believable example of Feedwizrd finding a loss that ROAS hides—and see the exact action they can take without surrendering account control—they will perceive the product as more valuable and safer than a generic profit dashboard.

## 2. Page Goals and Conversion Path

### Primary conversion

Send the visitor to account creation or Shopify installation.

**Working primary CTA:** `Get started`  
**Price-qualified CTA near pricing:** `Start for $29/month`  
**If a free trial is confirmed later:** Replace both with `Start free trial` and state the trial length and card requirement.

### Secondary conversion

Let uncertain visitors inspect the mechanism without connecting their accounts.

**Working secondary CTA:** `See how it works`  
**Behavior:** Scroll to the product demonstration rather than open a vague contact form.

If a demo store, interactive product tour, or recorded walkthrough becomes available, it should replace the scroll-only behavior.

### Conversion logic

1. Establish audience and outcome.
2. Prove that ROAS can hide a product loss.
3. Show Feedwizrd finding the loss in the product interface.
4. Show the improve/draft/exclude decision and approval step.
5. Explain setup and account control.
6. Add credible outcome proof.
7. Present an unambiguous $29 offer.
8. Resolve remaining objections.
9. Repeat the primary CTA.

## 3. Required Landing-Page Sections

The recommended page contains 13 core sections plus the header and footer.

### 0. Sticky Header

**Purpose:** Keep orientation and the primary action available without distracting from the page.

**Required content:**

- Feedwizrd logo
- Anchor links: Product, How it works, Pricing, FAQ
- Sign in link
- Primary CTA

**Desktop behavior:** Compact sticky navigation; CTA remains visible.  
**Mobile behavior:** Logo, primary CTA, and menu trigger. Use a simple full-height menu for anchors and Sign in. Do not hide the CTA inside the menu.

### 1. Hero: The Daily Profit Decision

**Purpose:** Establish relevance, explain the economic outcome, and present the product before the visitor has to scroll.

**Message job:**

- Identify Shopify + Google Ads users.
- Lead with product-level profit, not AI.
- Introduce the find/improve/monitor loop.
- Make the first action low-risk.

**Content requirements:**

- Audience eyebrow: Shopify sellers running Google Ads
- One benefit-led headline
- One short supporting paragraph
- Primary and secondary CTAs
- Pricing/reassurance microcopy: working start at $29/month, cancel term if confirmed
- Product-led visual showing a real product row, profit state, recommendation, and change history

**Working headline territory—not final copy:**

> Know which products make money. Fix the ones that do not.

**Visual brief:** Use a real or high-fidelity product interface rather than an abstract illustration. The visual should reveal the differentiated workflow, not merely show charts.

**Responsive behavior:**

- Desktop: 5/7 or 6/6 text-to-product split; keep the first product state visible without scrolling.
- Tablet: Text first, then a wide product panel.
- Mobile: One-column hero; CTAs stack full width; show a focused crop of one product card instead of shrinking an entire desktop dashboard.

### 2. Reassurance Strip

**Purpose:** Reduce immediate setup and access anxiety.

**Content candidates:**

- Connect Shopify + Google Ads
- About five minutes to set up
- No tracking code
- No changes without approval

Only publish claims that the product team confirms.

**Visual brief:** Four compact text-and-icon items. Do not use fake customer logos as a substitute for proof.

**Responsive behavior:** Two-by-two grid on mobile or horizontal swipe only if each item remains fully readable.

### 3. Problem Proof: The Product ROAS Hides

**Purpose:** Make the category problem concrete and economically urgent.

**Message job:** Demonstrate that an apparently healthy product can lose money after COGS, fees, and ad spend.

**Content requirements:**

- A short problem headline
- One realistic product example
- Side-by-side view: what Google/Shopify shows versus what Feedwizrd calculates
- A clear list of the profit inputs included
- A note that the example is illustrative if it is not customer data

**Visual brief:** A compact calculation or waterfall, not a paragraph. Example structure:

`Revenue → COGS → fees → ad spend → actual product profit`

**CRO rule:** Do not use the phrase “real profit” until the exact calculation and exclusions are visible.

**Responsive behavior:** Convert the desktop side-by-side comparison into two stacked cards. Keep arithmetic in a single readable vertical flow.

### 4. Product Demonstration: One Morning, One Decision

**Purpose:** Prove how Feedwizrd fits into the merchant's daily routine.

**Message job:** Show the actual dashboard and let the visitor understand the answer within seconds.

**Required product states:**

- Product list with profit status
- 7/14/30/60/90-day selector
- Revenue, cost, fees, ad spend, and profit breakdown
- Zombie-product filter
- A selected losing product
- Recommended next action

**Interaction option:** A lightweight three-state product demo:

1. All products
2. Losing product selected
3. Profit calculation expanded

If interaction is not feasible, use an annotated real screenshot with no more than four callouts.

**Responsive behavior:** On mobile, represent each product as a card. Keep profit status and next action visible; move detailed metrics into an expandable drawer.

### 5. Differentiator: Find → Fix → Verify

**Purpose:** Separate Feedwizrd from read-only analytics tools and from Pythago's general “find losers” narrative.

**Three stages:**

1. **Find:** Surface loss-making or stagnant products using product-level profit and the zombie filter.
2. **Fix:** Review an AI feed recommendation, draft the product in Shopify, or exclude it from the campaign.
3. **Verify:** Monitor the change over the next selected time window and compare the result.

**Content requirements:**

- What the merchant sees at each stage
- What action is possible
- What Feedwizrd records or monitors
- Explicit merchant-approval indicator

**Visual brief:** Use one connected timeline based on the same product throughout. Avoid three unrelated feature cards.

**Responsive behavior:** Horizontal timeline on desktop; vertical stepper on mobile. Maintain the same product identity through all three steps.

### 6. Capability Support: Everything Behind the Decision

**Purpose:** Cover feature completeness after the core value has been understood.

**Feature groups:**

- AI feed improvement
- 7/14/30/60/90-day analysis
- Feed zombie filter
- Multi-feed support
- Feed-change monitoring
- Full ROI/profit tracking
- Performance dashboard
- Multi-store support

**Content rule:** Organize features by outcome, not as a flat icon grid.

Suggested grouping:

- **See the truth:** Profit calculation, time windows, performance dashboard
- **Act sooner:** Zombie filter, recommendations, direct actions
- **Learn what worked:** Change history and monitoring
- **Scale the workflow:** Multi-feed and multi-store support

**Responsive behavior:** Two-column groups on desktop; stacked accordions or short cards on mobile. Do not hide essential features behind hover interactions.

### 7. How Setup and Daily Use Work

**Purpose:** Make adoption feel fast and operationally safe.

**Four-step sequence:**

1. Connect Shopify through its official flow.
2. Connect Google Ads through Google's official flow.
3. Feedwizrd synchronizes orders, costs, fees, and ad spend.
4. Review product profit and take a merchant-approved action.

**Content requirements:**

- Approximate setup time
- No tracking-code requirement
- Expected time until useful data appears
- Historical data availability
- Supported account/campaign conditions

**Visual brief:** Use recognizable connection states and short product screenshots. Avoid generic numbered circles without evidence.

**Responsive behavior:** Four-step horizontal flow on desktop; vertical progression on mobile.

### 8. Trust and Account Control

**Purpose:** Resolve the highest-risk conversion concern before pricing.

**Message territory:** Your accounts, your call.

**Required content:**

- Shopify permissions requested
- Google Ads permissions requested
- What Feedwizrd can read
- What Feedwizrd can change
- What Feedwizrd cannot change
- Automations off by default
- Preview/approval behavior
- Access revocation method
- Data storage, encryption, retention, and deletion

**Visual brief:** Use a permission matrix with three labels: Read, Change with approval, Never access.

**CRO rule:** Do not reduce this to security badges. Plain-language scope is more persuasive.

**Responsive behavior:** Convert the matrix into expandable permission cards on mobile. The labels must remain visible without opening each card.

### 9. Outcome Proof

**Purpose:** Replace vendor claims with credible evidence.

**Preferred proof format:** One mini case study based on a real product decision.

**Case-study structure:**

1. Store type and relevant scale
2. Product appeared acceptable by ROAS
3. Feedwizrd exposed the actual profit loss
4. Merchant-approved action taken
5. Measured result over a defined time period

**Secondary proof:** Customer quote, product screenshot, and permission to identify the customer or store.

**Launch fallback if no customer proof exists:**

- Use a transparent, labeled product walkthrough.
- Use founder/operator credibility only if it is documented.
- Do not publish “most users” or average-savings claims until substantiated.

**Responsive behavior:** One primary case study, not a tiny multi-card carousel. Stack result metrics under the story on mobile.

### 10. Pricing

**Purpose:** Make cost and plan eligibility immediately understandable.

**Working public message:** Starting at $29/month.

**Critical pricing rule:** Only use that exact claim if a customer can purchase a plan for a total of $29/month. If $29 applies only to each store on a 3+ store plan, say:

> From $29 per store/month with 3+ stores.

Do not imply a $29 total if the minimum charge is $49 or $87.

**Recommended pricing presentation:**

- One simple self-serve plan or a compact store-count selector
- Store count and total monthly cost shown together
- All included capabilities summarized once
- Enterprise route for 10+ stores
- Billing cycle, cancellation terms, taxes, and trial terms if applicable
- Email support inclusion

**Current pricing inputs requiring reconciliation:**

- Working start: $29/month
- Earlier 1-store price: $49/month
- Earlier 2-store price: $39/store/month
- Earlier 3+ store price: $29/store/month
- Enterprise: price not supplied

**Responsive behavior:** Avoid a four-column pricing table. Use a store-count selector and one active price summary, or stack plan cards vertically with the recommended plan first.

### 11. FAQ

**Purpose:** Resolve final objections without forcing visitors into support conversations.

**Required questions:**

1. How is Feedwizrd's profit calculated?
2. How is this different from the numbers in Shopify and Google Ads?
3. Does Feedwizrd change products or campaigns automatically?
4. What can Feedwizrd access in my accounts?
5. Can I undo a feed or campaign change?
6. How does the zombie-product filter work?
7. What does AI feed improvement change?
8. Does it work for one store?
9. Does it support multiple feeds, markets, and currencies?
10. How long does setup and initial synchronization take?
11. What happens to my data if I disconnect or cancel?
12. How does pricing work for several stores?

**Content rule:** Use direct answers. Link to longer security or help documentation only after giving the useful answer.

**Responsive behavior:** Accessible single-open accordion. Questions and focus states must remain keyboard friendly.

### 12. Final CTA

**Purpose:** Convert visitors after the complete argument has been made.

**Content requirements:**

- Restate the find/fix/verify outcome
- Starting price or confirmed trial offer
- No-code and approval reassurance
- Primary CTA
- Secondary contact or demo option only if operationally supported

**Working message territory:**

> Find the product costing you money before another week of ad spend does.

Do not present this as a guaranteed outcome.

**Responsive behavior:** Centered desktop block; stacked mobile CTA with a persistent bottom action after the section enters view.

### 13. Footer

**Purpose:** Provide navigation, legal reassurance, and product access without adding another sales narrative.

**Required content:**

- Short product descriptor
- Product, Pricing, FAQ, Sign in
- Contact/support email
- Privacy Policy, Terms, Cookie Policy
- Security or data-processing link
- Company details and copyright

## 5. Page-Level Responsive Requirements

### Layout system

- Design mobile-first.
- Use a 12-column desktop grid, 8-column tablet grid, and 4-column mobile grid.
- Recommended content width: 1160–1240 px.
- Use generous vertical spacing but keep the first proof point close to the hero.
- Avoid desktop-only compositions that turn into illegible screenshots on mobile.

### Suggested breakpoints

- Mobile: 320–767 px
- Tablet: 768–1023 px
- Desktop: 1024 px and above
- Large desktop: optimize the content width rather than stretching layouts indefinitely

### Mobile conversion behavior

- Keep the header CTA visible.
- Introduce a bottom sticky CTA only after the hero has scrolled away.
- Hide it while the pricing CTA or final CTA is visible to avoid duplication.
- Use full-width buttons with at least 44 px touch height.
- Preserve essential product status and actions; place secondary metrics behind disclosure.
- Do not use horizontal comparison tables.

### Product visuals

- Use real interface states at 2× resolution.
- Create dedicated mobile crops rather than scaling desktop screenshots.
- Every screenshot needs a legible caption or annotation.
- Avoid autoplay video with sound.
- Lazy-load media below the initial viewport.

### Accessibility

- Meet WCAG AA contrast for text, controls, and status indicators.
- Do not communicate profitable/unprofitable status by color alone.
- Provide visible keyboard focus states.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Use semantic headings in the same order as this brief.
- Provide alt text that explains what a product screenshot demonstrates.

## 6. Content and Asset Requirements

The design should not begin with final fidelity until these assets exist or have approved placeholders.

### Product assets

- Dashboard screenshot with product-level profit
- Profit-input breakdown
- Zombie-filter state
- AI feed recommendation with explanation
- Approval/preview state
- Draft/exclude action state
- Feed-change history
- Before/after monitoring view
- Shopify and Google connection states

### Trust assets

- Exact Shopify permission list
- Exact Google Ads permission list
- Data storage and deletion statement
- Security and encryption facts
- Cancellation and access-revocation flow

### Proof assets

- At least one documented customer case
- Customer permission to publish name/logo or a justified anonymized format
- Baseline and result dates
- Profit/savings calculation method
- Unedited supporting screenshot where possible

### Commercial assets

- Final $29 plan definition
- Store counts and total monthly prices
- Enterprise offer
- Trial or guarantee decision
- Billing and cancellation terms

## 7. Copy Guardrails

### Use

- Specific product and money language
- Short explanations of why familiar metrics mislead
- Plain-language cost inputs
- Concrete approved actions
- Merchant control and reversibility
- A daily workflow rather than a long feature inventory

### Avoid

- “All-in-one growth platform”
- “Scale with confidence” and similar category clichés
- Leading with AI
- Calling the data real-time unless refresh frequency is confirmed
- Claiming guaranteed savings or profitability
- Unsubstantiated “most users” statistics
- Pythago-like headline, section, CTA, or founder-story wording
- Using “from $29/month” if $29 is not the lowest purchasable total

## 8. SEO and Share Metadata Direction

**Working title:** Feedwizrd — Product Profit Tracking for Shopify Google Ads  
**Working meta description:** See profit by product, improve weak feeds, and monitor every change across Shopify and Google Ads. Feedwizrd plans start at $29/month.

Update the price language if the $29 plan definition changes.

### Primary search territory

- Shopify product profit tracking
- Google Ads profit tracking for Shopify
- Product-level Google Ads profitability
- Shopify product feed optimization

SEO language should remain secondary to conversion clarity in the hero.

## 10. Pre-Design Decisions Required

These decisions affect page structure or message accuracy and should be resolved before final copy and high-fidelity design:

1. Is $29/month a purchasable one-store plan or only a per-store volume rate?
2. Is there a free trial, demo account, recorded demo, or money-back guarantee?
3. What exact costs are included in product profit?
4. What qualifies a product as a zombie?
5. What feed attributes can the AI improve?
6. Can a user preview and undo every change?
7. What does Feedwizrd's monitoring prove after a change?
8. What public customer evidence is available?
9. What exact Shopify and Google permissions are requested?
10. Which markets, currencies, campaign types, and feeds are supported?

## 11. Final Design Principle

The page should leave the visitor with one clear mental model:

> Feedwizrd does not just tell me a product is losing money. It helps me decide what to change, keeps me in control, and shows me what happened next.
